## Advice for flourishing at the SAGE Lab

Planning a PhD or PsyD application? Our [graduate application guide]({{< relref "preparing-graduate-applications.md" >}}) brings together advice on grades, research, helping experience, funding and publications. If you need to explore other research opportunities, see [accessing research experience]({{< relref "research-experience-access.md" >}}).

### Protect your academic record

Aim for the best grades you can in every course, ideally A+, while protecting your well-being. Keep time for studying before adding research commitments. Strong academic results matter for many doctoral admissions and scholarships. For example, [UQAR’s professional doctorate in psychology](https://www.uqar.ca/programmes-domaines-detudes/doctorat-en-psychologie-cheminement-professionnel/) considers academic results alongside research experience and other criteria. Grades strengthen a dossier without guaranteeing admission.

### Apply for funding

Look for every funding opportunity you are eligible for and aim to apply to each relevant one. Check eligibility, fit, documents and deadlines early so applications fit alongside your courses and well-being. Start with the [UQAR scholarship directory](https://etudes.uqar.ca/bourses) and ask about the lab’s student-funding resources.

Funding can support you financially, make research feasible and strengthen your CV. Awards also count among the achievements considered in some admissions and funding evaluations, including the [FRQ doctoral scholarship criteria](https://frq.gouv.qc.ca/programme/bourses-de-doctorat-en-recherche-2027-2028-b2-volet-regulier-et-volet-reintegration-a-la-recherche/). Criteria vary by program. We want the SAGE Lab to build a strong funding record by sharing what we learn. Funding applications are one of Rémi’s strengths and a skill we can develop together. Bring a prepared draft and specific questions when asking for feedback.

### Develop autonomy and ask for help thoughtfully

Look for ways to learn and move projects forward with limited demands on Rémi’s time. Consult documentation, try a bounded first step and seek suitable peer feedback. When asking Rémi for help, summarize what you tried, what remains unclear and which decision you need. More autonomous contributions can make it feasible to support more people.

Ask early when you are genuinely blocked or a decision affects ethics, data access or scientific validity. Autonomy includes recognizing when supervision is necessary. The research supervisor remains responsible for the guidance and review agreed for your role.

### Help bring research to publication

When you start or join a research project, commit to helping bring it to scientific publication, including careful revisions when the work takes time or becomes challenging. Agree on your role, the intended manuscript, your deliverables and a continuation plan. Publication is a shared goal.

If circumstances change, discuss them early and arrange a documented handover. Protecting well-being may require rescoping or ending participation responsibly. Keep your contribution usable by the next person. Publication depends on the work and journal decisions, and authorship depends on actual contributions. A bounded training exercise does not automatically become a paper or an open-ended obligation.

### Choose projects that fit our resources

Consider existing grants, data and supervision before proposing new data collection. Options requiring little or no additional spending still need expertise, time and review. Examples to discuss include:

- Analyses of suitable public or existing data, including material on the [Open Science Framework (OSF)](https://osf.io/).
- A carefully scoped meta-analysis or theoretical review.
- Metascience research, such as a question connected with the Missing Majority Dashboard, if its data and methods support it.
- Reproduction or replication work, potentially with the [Institute for Replication](https://www.i4replication.org/), when skills and supervision fit.

These are possibilities, not approved projects. A useful proposal identifies the question, available materials, likely output and help required.




## Before your first project meeting

Bring your questions, realistic availability and one skill you want to develop. Together, confirm your supervising contact, first deliverable, feedback arrangements and where to save your work. Identify the key papers for your project and agree on a useful stopping point and handover plan. Document files, scripts, sources, decisions and unfinished work from the beginning.

**Our common aim: warmth and competence, self-compassion and excellence.** We want you to learn, do work you can trust and build a sustainable place in research.
