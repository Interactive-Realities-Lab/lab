The Interactive Realities Laboratory (IRLab) is a human-computer interaction research lab at Iowa State University directed by Dr. Regis Kopper. We study extended reality (XR), 3D user interfaces, immersive simulation and training, and the user experience of virtual and augmented reality systems.

IRLab works on both basic interaction problems and applied XR systems. Our work includes the design and evaluation of interaction techniques, tangible and embodied interfaces, simulation of future user interfaces, and immersive technologies for complex real-world settings.

## 3D Interaction and XR User Experience

We study how people select, navigate, manipulate, communicate, and make decisions in immersive environments. Our work aims to improve the usability, precision, comfort, and effectiveness of 3D user interfaces.

## XR Training and Simulation

We build immersive simulations for studying human performance and for training in complex scenarios. VR also provides a platform for prototyping and evaluating interfaces and technologies that may not yet exist as deployable systems.

## XR for Public Safety

We investigate augmented and virtual reality interfaces for first responders, with attention to situational awareness, decision support, training, and integration with existing operational procedures.

{{< research_image src="media/research/traffic-stop-ar.webp" alt="Augmented reality interface for a public-safety traffic-stop scenario" >}}

## XR for Healthcare

We explore immersive technologies for healthcare training, medical visualization, remote expert guidance, and more accessible delivery of expertise.

{{< research_image src="media/research/doctor-training-ar.webp" alt="Augmented reality for medical training" >}}

## Tangible and Embodied Interaction

We study how physical objects, tangible devices, and body movement can improve interaction with virtual and augmented environments.
